# RupeeRise: Fact-Checking an AI-Written Investor Brief

**A FinTech case study on catching AI hallucinations before they reach investors.**

Author: Anshika · BBA (FinTech), Chitkara Business School · Tools: Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity · November 2026

## The case
**RupeeRise** (fictional) is a digital lending app in Bengaluru. Its founder asked AI to write an investor brief on India's digital lending and UPI market and wanted to send it as it was. As a Business Analyst, I checked every claim against official sources before it could reach investors.

## What I did

| Step | Task | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generated two demonstration briefs | Claude, ChatGPT (constructed examples) |
| 2 | Scored both drafts on 5 criteria | Manual |
| 3 | Listed 10 claims and assessed confidence, recency risk and verification sources | AI-style self-check + manual |
| 4 | Verified every claim against RBI, NPCI and PIB | Official websites |
| 5 | Audited official sources and checked whether they supported the claims | Manual source audit |

## Worst error I found

The AI-generated brief claimed that the RBI permits digital lenders to collect customers' full phone contact lists for credit scoring. However, RBI's Digital Lending Directions, 2025, Paragraph 12 restricts the collection and use of personal data. Access to contact lists and call logs is not permitted as a general credit-scoring practice. This could seriously mislead investors about regulatory compliance and create concerns about customer privacy.

## My top 3 "What not to do" rules

1. Never send an AI-generated number to investors without checking the primary source (evidence: C01).
2. Always read the actual RBI regulatory paragraph before explaining a rule (evidence: C06–C08).
3. Never publish a quotation without an original and verifiable source (evidence: C09).

## My answer to the founder

No, the original AI-generated brief should not be sent to investors as it is. Incorrect regulatory claims, unsupported market figures and unverified quotations must first be corrected or removed. The brief should also undergo independent fact-checking and legal review before external circulation.

## What I learned

- AI writes confident, fluent text even when facts are wrong, outdated or made up.
- Exact numbers, circular numbers, quotes and sources are where AI makes the most mistakes.
- AI's own confidence rating is not proof; only an official source is.
- A cited link is not proof either: it must be opened and must actually support the claim.
- Different AI tools give different answers to the same prompt, so tools must be compared fairly.
- For Indian finance, the official sources are RBI, NPCI, PIB and company reports.
- Nothing AI-written should reach investors or customers without a human check.
